## 01 · Welcome: how the website works
*≈181 words · ≈75 seconds*

Welcome to the Frontier Perspectives website. In this short series you'll learn how to run everything on the site yourself — no technical knowledge needed. Let's start with what visitors see. At the top is the menu: research articles, jobs, fellowships, scholarships, grants and the About page.

The homepage shows our headline, the latest articles and live counts of open opportunities. Here's the Jobs page. Every job you add in the admin appears here automatically, sorted by deadline — the soonest first. Visitors can filter by type, field, location and country with these buttons.

And here's the best part: when a deadline passes, the listing disappears from these lists by itself. You never have to remember to take things down. Each listing has its own page with the details and an Apply button that goes to the official website. Fellowships, scholarships and grants work the same way.

Visitors can also send us opportunities with the Submit Opportunity form — those arrive in your admin as messages. In the next videos we'll go behind the scenes, starting with how to log in.

## 02 · Logging in, your password and staying safe
*≈144 words · ≈60 seconds*

To manage the website, go to frontierperspectives dot com slash wp-admin. Bookmark this page. Enter your username or email and your password, and click Log In. If you ever forget your password, click 'Lost your password?' and you'll get a reset link by email. For security, the site locks a login for twenty minutes after several wrong passwords — so if you see that message, simply wait and try again.

To change your password, click your name at the top right, then Edit Profile. Scroll down to Account Management and click Set New Password. WordPress suggests a strong password — you can keep it or type your own, then click Update Profile. A few safety tips: never share your password by email, give each helper their own login instead of sharing yours, and always log out on shared computers — top right, Log Out.

## 03 · A tour of the admin
*≈184 words · ≈76 seconds*

This is the admin dashboard. Everything is in the menu on the left. Articles is for research articles, Publications for journals and reports, Jobs and Fellowships and Scholarships and Grants for listings. Authors are the people on the About page and article bylines.

Hero Slides control the big box on the homepage. Messages is your inbox from the website form. Media holds all uploaded images and PDFs, Pages has the simple pages like About, and Site Settings holds your logo, contact email and social links. Let's open Jobs and Fellowships.

Every job is a row in this table. Use the search box to find one, and these links to see only published items, drafts or the trash. The Deadline column shows when each closes — a red 'Expired' label means the deadline has passed and the job is already hidden from the public site.

Hover over a row to see the actions: edit, quick edit, trash, view the live page, and duplicate — very handy for similar listings. To jump to the website at any time, click the site name at the top left.

## 04 · Adding a job
*≈215 words · ≈89 seconds*

Let's add a job. Go to Jobs and Fellowships, then Add New. First the title — the position name exactly as you'd like visitors to see it. Below it is the text box. Paste the full job description here — formatting from Word or the original website is kept. To turn a line into a heading, use the Paragraph menu in the toolbar.

Now scroll down to the Details box. Institution is required — that's the employer. Upload the institution's logo: click Choose or upload, Upload files, select the image, and Choose file. Location is free text, as you want it shown. Country or region decides which country page it appears on.

Tick Remote OK only if the job can be done remotely. Position type matters: anything ending in 'Fellowship' also appears on the Fellowships page. Field or discipline becomes a filter on the Jobs page automatically. Salary is optional. Then the application deadline — pick the date.

If there's no fixed deadline, leave it empty and type something like 'Open until filled' in the Deadline note. Finally, the application link — the official page where people apply. Click Publish. That's it — the job is live. Here it is on the Jobs page, sorted by deadline, with its field already available as a filter.

## 05 · Adding a fellowship
*≈81 words · ≈34 seconds*

Fellowships are added exactly like jobs — the only difference is the Position type. I'll save time by duplicating an existing listing: hover it and click Duplicate. A draft copy opens. I change the title, and set Position type to one of the Fellowship types — here, Visiting Fellowship.

Publish. Now it appears on the Fellowships page, and under Fellowships, Visiting Fellowships in the menu. It also stays on the main Jobs page, because fellowships are a kind of academic position.

## 06 · Adding a scholarship or grant
*≈128 words · ≈53 seconds*

Scholarships and grants live under Scholarships and Grants. Click Add New, give it a title and paste the description. In Details, the Funder is required — the organisation offering it — and you can upload their logo. Level or type decides where it appears: BS/MS, PhD and Post Doc are scholarships.

Any level with 'Grant' in its name also appears on the Grants page. Eligible regions are who can apply — one per line. These become the Region filter for visitors. Deadline, deadline note and application link work just like jobs. Publish. Here it is on the Scholarships page, and the Region filter already includes 'Africa'.

Now a grant: I'll duplicate it and change the level to Research Grant. After publishing, it appears on the Grants page too.

## 07 · Deadlines: how listings expire automatically
*≈129 words · ≈53 seconds*

Here's how deadlines work, because it saves you a lot of work. A listing stays on the website through the whole of its deadline day — visitors see 'Closes today' in red. Listings closing within two weeks are highlighted too. After midnight, the listing disappears from every public list automatically: jobs, fellowships, country pages, search and the homepage counts.

But nothing is deleted. If someone has an old link, the page still opens and clearly says the opportunity has closed. In the admin you'll see a red Expired label. If a deadline is extended, just change the date and click Update — it's back immediately. And for listings with no fixed date, leave the deadline empty and use the Deadline note instead; they stay listed until you unpublish them.

## 08 · Editing, extending, duplicating and removing listings
*≈112 words · ≈46 seconds*

To change a listing, hover it and click Edit, make the change, and click Update. For quick fixes to the title or status, use Quick Edit right in the list. Duplicate is perfect when an institution posts several similar positions. To take something offline without deleting it, switch it to draft.

To do many at once, tick them, choose Unpublish in Bulk actions, and click Apply. Deleting moves an item to the Trash. You can restore it from the Trash for thirty days. A tip: for filled positions, it's better to set the deadline to yesterday than to delete — old links keep working and Google is told the job has closed.

## 09 · Writing and publishing an article
*≈154 words · ≈64 seconds*

Let's publish a research article. Articles, Add New. Type the headline as the title, then write or paste the article. The toolbar works like Word: use the Paragraph menu to make section headings, and the buttons for bullet lists and quotes — this makes long articles easy to read and helps Google understand them.

Below, in Details: the standfirst is a one-to-three sentence summary shown under the headline and on article cards. Choose the author. Add a main image — then describe it in the 'alt' box for blind readers and search engines — plus a caption and photo credit if needed.

Key findings appear as a highlighted box at the top. One finding per line. Leave it empty if you don't want the box. In the right-hand column, tick a category, and add a few tags. Click Preview to check it, then Publish. Here it is, live, with the reading time calculated automatically.

## 10 · Images, links, videos and tables inside articles
*≈91 words · ≈38 seconds*

Inside an article you can add more than text. Click where the picture should go, then Add Media, upload the image, describe it in the Alternative Text box, and click Insert. To make a link, select the words, click the link icon, and paste the address. To embed a video, just paste the YouTube link on its own line; it becomes a player.

And for a table, make it in Word or Google Docs, copy it, and paste it into the article — the table is kept. Click Update to save.

## 11 · Scheduling an article
*≈49 words · ≈20 seconds*

You can prepare articles in advance. In the Publish box, next to 'Publish immediately', click Edit, set the date and time, and click OK. The Publish button changes to Schedule. Click it, and the article goes live by itself at that moment. In the list it shows as Scheduled.

## 12 · Categories and tags
*≈52 words · ≈22 seconds*

Categories group your articles — like Climate, Economy or Education — and appear as filter buttons on the Research Articles page once an article uses them. Add or rename them under Articles, Categories. Tags are smaller labels shown at the end of an article. Use a handful of categories, and tags freely.

## 13 · Publications
*≈102 words · ≈42 seconds*

Publications are your journals, reports and special editions. Add New, give it a title and a description. In Details, set the edition — for example Volume 2, Issue 1 — and the type. Upload a portrait cover image and the PDF itself. Add the ISSN if it has one, and the table of contents, one entry per line.

Contributors and articles are chosen from lists: hold Control, or Command on a Mac, to select several. Tick 'Feature on homepage' to make it the homepage highlight when there's no hero slide. Publish — visitors can now read the description and download the PDF.

*≈62 words · ≈26 seconds*

Authors are the people behind the site. Each author has a name, a short bio, a role and a photo. Published authors appear in the Our Team section of the About page, and can be picked as an article's author or a publication's contributor. To remove someone from the About page, switch them to draft — their past articles keep their name.

## 15 · Changing the homepage feature box
*≈87 words · ≈36 seconds*

The big box on the right of the homepage is controlled by Hero Slides. It shows one slide — the one with the lowest Order number. Edit it: the title is the headline, the eyebrow is the small orange label above it, and the link target is where it goes when clicked — for example slash articles slash the article's address.

Upload a wide image and click Update. To feature a publication instead, switch all hero slides to draft and tick 'Feature on homepage' on the publication.

## 16 · Logo, tagline, contact email, footer, social links
*≈76 words · ≈31 seconds*

Site-wide details are in two places. Under Settings, General you'll find the Site Title and the Tagline — the tagline is the big headline on the homepage. Everything else is under Site Settings: the logo, the contact email shown on the site, the mission statement used in the footer, homepage and About page, and your social media links — one per line, the name, a vertical bar, then the address.

Save, and the whole site updates.

## 17 · Editing pages: About, Submit, Privacy, Terms, new pages
*≈86 words · ≈36 seconds*

Simple pages are under Pages. On the About page, any text you add appears under the mission quote. The Privacy Policy and Terms of Use were prepared as drafts. Read them, adjust anything that doesn't fit — ideally have them checked — and publish. The links appear in the footer automatically.

You can also create new pages, like an FAQ, with Add New. Note: adding a page to the main menu is a small design change — see the help video on getting help from AI.

## 18 · Reading and handling messages
*≈68 words · ≈28 seconds*

When someone uses the Submit Opportunity form, the message lands in Messages — the number shows how many are unread. Click a message to read it; the sender's email is on the right — click it to reply from your email. When it's an opportunity worth listing, add it under Jobs or Scholarships, then trash the message.

The form has built-in spam protection, so you'll rarely see junk.

## 19 · Adding users and roles
*≈60 words · ≈25 seconds*

Never share your own password. Instead, give each helper their own account: Users, Add New, fill in their username and email, and choose a role. Editor can manage all content but not the site settings — ideal for a research assistant. Author can only write their own articles.

When someone leaves, delete their account and assign their content to you.

## 20 · Google: SEO box, sharing images, Search Console
*≈107 words · ≈44 seconds*

The site is already set up for Google: page titles, descriptions, a sitemap, and special job information so listings can appear in Google's job search. For important articles you can fine-tune how they appear: in the SEO box, write a title of about sixty characters and a description of about one hundred and fifty.

The social share image is what LinkedIn or WhatsApp shows when someone shares the link. One setting to know: under Settings, Reading, 'Discourage search engines' must stay unticked, or Google will hide the site. Finally, connect Google Search Console and submit the sitemap — the address is your domain slash wp-sitemap dot xml.

## 21 · Monthly maintenance: updates and backups
*≈61 words · ≈25 seconds*

Once a month, spend ten minutes on maintenance. Security updates for WordPress install themselves, but check Dashboard, Updates for plugin updates. Update them one at a time, and after each, check the homepage, a job page and the form. Every six months, make a backup in Hostinger: in hPanel, Websites, Backups, create a backup, and download a copy to your computer.

## 22 · Undo, restore and the Trash
*≈55 words · ≈23 seconds*

Mistakes are easy to fix. While typing, press Control Z — or show the second toolbar row and click the undo arrow. For older changes, click Browse next to Revisions, slide back to an earlier version, and restore it. And anything deleted sits in the Trash for thirty days — open Trash and click Restore.

---

## 23 · When something goes wrong
*≈86 words · ≈36 seconds*

If a change doesn't appear, refresh with Control plus F5 — browsers sometimes show an old copy. If website emails don't arrive, the messages are still safe in the Messages section; email sending needs a one-time setup described in the guide. If you're locked out, wait twenty minutes, or log in through Hostinger: in hPanel, open WordPress and click Login.

If you ever see 'critical error', it's usually a plugin update — deactivate the latest one in Hostinger, and contact a developer with the architecture file.

*≈77 words · ≈32 seconds*

For anything not covered in these videos, you have a technical handbook written for AI assistants. Open the docs folder, copy the prompt from the AI help file, then open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, attach the admin guide and the architecture file, paste the prompt and ask your question in plain English.
